QUESTIONS PARENTS ASK

Everything before you register.

What exactly is Nesis?

A three-week, instructor-led holiday programme (Aug 24 – Sep 12) for young people aged 10–17. Students join live classes, complete practical activities, work on a final project, and receive a certificate plus a parent progress report — not a bundle of pre-recorded videos.

How is this different from free content on YouTube?

Structure, instruction and accountability. A real instructor teaches live, your child interacts with other students, and every programme ends in a project they build and present — with someone checking in on whether they actually did it.

Do I need to install anything?

No. Registration and payment happen right on this page, and classes run on Google Meet or Zoom, using tools you likely already have. Class updates go out over WhatsApp.

What does my child walk away with?

A finished project in their chosen skill, a certificate of completion, and a short progress report covering attendance, activities completed, and strengths to build on.

How do I register and pay?

Pick a programme and click Register. Fill in your and your child's details, and you'll be shown our account details to pay the ₦50,000 fee by direct bank transfer — there's no card checkout. After paying, upload your proof of payment on the same page; that step is compulsory. Once we verify it (usually within 24 hours), your Register button turns into a Join Class button linking to that programme's WhatsApp group.

Is the fee the same for every programme?

Yes — ₦50,000 covers any one of the five programmes in full, including the certificate and parent progress report. Registration and onboarding close on 22 August.

What if my proof of payment hasn't been verified yet?

Your Register button will show as pending on this page while we check it — you don't need to do anything else. If there's an issue with the proof you uploaded, we'll ask you to re-upload it, and you can also message us on WhatsApp with your reference code if you'd like an update sooner.
